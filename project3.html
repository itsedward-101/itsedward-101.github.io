Predicting Credit Card Approval

A predictive analytics framework for estimating approval outcomes, ranking drivers, and stress-testing fairness with interpretability for credit decision support.

BANA 273 -> Machine Learning Analytics, UC Irvine

690 applications in dataset
10 models compared
Fβ threshold tuning (β = 2)

The Problem

Financial institutions process thousands of credit applications manually. This project builds a predictive analytics framework to estimate whether a credit card application will be approved, identify the strongest approval drivers, and translate findings into actionable targeting insights ? while maintaining interpretability for credit decision support.

The Data

Dataset
Credit Card Approval Dataset (Kaggle)
Observations
690
Variables
16
Target
Approved (binary: 1 = approved, 0 = denied)
Class split
~44.5% approved / ~55.5% denied
Key predictors
PriorDefault, CreditScore, Income, Debt, YearsEmployed, Employed, BankCustomer, Industry, Ethnicity

Feature Engineering

  • Removed low-signal variables: Age, Gender, DriversLicense, ZipCode, MaritalStatus, Citizenship
  • Credit score binning: decision-tree-based binning into 4 interpretable tiers (Poor, Low, Moderate, High)
  • Income transformation: log transformation to reduce skew from 13.14 to 0.51
  • Interaction terms: retained Debt Moderate Credit Score and YearsEmployed Log Income after likelihood ratio testing

Models Compared (10 total)

Baseline Logistic Regression, LASSO, Ridge, Elastic Net, Naive Bayes, Decision Tree, Random Forest, XGBoost, SVM Linear, and SVM RBF.

Threshold tuning: Used F-beta (β = 2) cross-validated threshold optimization to prioritize recall of approved applicants over the default 0.5 cutoff.

Fairness Analysis

Latino applicants had a raw approval rate of ~14% vs ~42% for White applicants.

After controlling for financial variables, Latino coefficient = 1.385, odds ratio = 0.25, p = 0.019.

Conclusion: Disparity exists in observed outcomes; financial profile differences explain part of the gap, but a residual signal remains ? warrants further review before production deployment.

Industry: Disparities also found: Utilities (~84% approval) vs Healthcare (~13%) ? largely explained by underlying financial profiles.
